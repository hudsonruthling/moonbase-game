Where the science comes from

The facts in “Did you know?” boxes come from these NASA sources. The game’s numbers are simplified so kids can play it, and lunar days are squeezed short. No live NASA feed is used: storm days are random but follow the real idea that the Sun is stormier at solar maximum. The 1972 storm fact is archived history, not live data.

  1. NASA, “Living and Working on the Moon”
  2. NASA Science, “NASA’s Moon Orbiter Spots New, ‘Once-in-Century’ Moon Crater,” Sep 16, 2026
  3. NASA, “Lunar Living: NASA’s Artemis Base Camp Concept,” Oct 28, 2020
  4. NASA, “Moon Base,” updated Nov 8, 2026
  5. NASA Science, “How NASA Will Protect Astronauts From Space Radiation at the Moon,” 2019 (updated 2026)
  6. K. Ryder and M. Campola, “The Lunar Radiation Environment,” NASA GSFC, 2022

Communication delay is our own calculation (240,000 miles at light speed, about 1.3 seconds one way), not a NASA quote.

Plan your mission

You lead 4 astronauts at the Moon’s south pole. Keep air, food and power up, keep radiation dose under 100, and do at least 5 science walks in 12 days.
